Block 25

Context
Final year project
My role
Research, branding concept, video editing
Team
With Tan Helen Hosea (game design, renders)
Output
Game concept, brand identity, explainer video

The problem

Void decks in Singapore's public housing estates are shared by elderly residents and young children every day, but their different routines mean they use the space side by side rather than together.

The question

How might void decks encourage shared moments between the generations, while respecting their everyday routines?

The game

Block 25 turns snakes and ladders into a life-size board on the void deck floor. Children play physically, stepping across the squares and rolling the dice. Elderly players scan a QR code to open a "magic lens" on their phone, which reveals a hidden layer of risks and shortcuts.

Only the senior can see the danger, and only the child can move, so the game moves forward through talking and trust. Does the child listen when guided? Does the senior allow moments of risk and fun?

My part

I led the research and developed the Block 25 brand, from the playful dice logo to its colour palette, then edited the explainer video that walks through how the game is played.

BranchOut!

Client
Woodlands Botanic Garden, Singapore
Brief
Attract and engage more youth visitors
My role
Research, A1 presentation board, visual assets for the booklet and booth
Audience
Lower secondary students, 12 to 15

The brief

Woodlands Botanic Garden asked us to bring in more young visitors. We looked at how nature could feel more appealing and accessible to younger audiences, focusing on lower secondary students.

Research insight

Students spend most of their time in screen-based routines and rigid academic structures, with little outdoor learning, and that distance dulls both curiosity and a sense of responsibility for the environment. A survey by Channel News Asia found 62% never go on nature walks. So we asked: how might we bring nature into the lives of young people and their support networks in a meaningful way?

Our response

BranchOut! is a hands-on learning experience built around four curated paths through the garden: Butterfly, Flower, Hidden and Wild Bites. Each comes with an activity booklet for exploring, completing tasks and recording discoveries.

Finishing a path earns a stamp, and a take-home activity, like raising a caterpillar, earns a second. A return visit unlocks a path keychain, so one school trip can grow into a habit.

My part

I contributed to the research, and was primarily responsible for the design and layout of the A1 presentation board, making sure it communicated our research and concept clearly. I also helped refine the visual assets across our other deliverables, including the activity booklet and booth setup.

Pathfind

Client
Science Centre Singapore
Context
DP3612 Design Studio 3, project pitch
My role
Research
Team
With Helen and Mengyang (signage design, renders)
Output
Wayfinding system, maps, lighting

The problem

The Kinetic Garden is meant to promote outdoor learning, but visitors struggle to find it or find their way around it. By day, the layout is scattered, main and side paths look alike, and nothing points the way from the Science Centre. At night, paths, crossings and stairs are poorly lit, which is a real safety concern for children and older visitors.

The approach

Looking at how Gardens by the Bay uses low path lights and lit signage, we designed a three-level system that works both day and night.

Main maps and directional boards sit at the entrances. Tall illuminated path guides carry a map along the routes. Low intersection guides mark each junction, paired with glowing path edges.

My part

I led the research: observing the garden by day and by night, documenting confusing paths, missing signs and poorly lit areas, and studying Gardens by the Bay as a precedent. These findings shaped the problem statement and the three-level system.

Rebuilding Borough

Context
International placement, University of the Arts London
Brief
Trace
Team
Individual project
Output
30–45 second moving image, A1 poster

The brief

During a two-month placement at UAL, the brief Trace asked us to observe and represent a movement or pattern inside one of London's markets, through a short moving image and a printed piece.

Approach

I chose Borough Market for its mix of tourists and locals, its sensory density, and its closeness to the school. Working from R. Murray Schafer's soundscape framework, I sorted what I heard into three layers: constant ambience, specific cues, and rhythmic combinations.

That breakdown became the structure of the film. It rebuilds the market's atmosphere from layers of familiar and unnoticed sounds, pairing them with experimental editing to push past a conventional video format.

The poster

For the exhibition I designed an A1 poster that turns the soundscape into image. Each sound quote sits where its source was in the market, mapped onto a photograph of Borough Market.

What I took from it

The project grew my understanding of place-based narrative and my interest in multi-sensory storytelling through design.
