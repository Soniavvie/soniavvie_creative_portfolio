The problem

The Kinetic Garden is meant to promote outdoor learning, but visitors struggle to find it or find their way around it. By day, the layout is scattered, main and side paths look alike, and nothing points the way from the Science Centre. At night, paths, crossings and stairs are poorly lit, which is a real safety concern for children and older visitors.

The approach

Looking at how Gardens by the Bay uses low path lights and lit signage, we designed a three-level system that works both day and night.

Main maps and directional boards sit at the entrances. Tall illuminated path guides carry a map along the routes. Low intersection guides mark each junction, paired with glowing path edges.

My part

I led the research: observing the garden by day and by night, documenting confusing paths, missing signs and poorly lit areas, and studying Gardens by the Bay as a precedent. These findings shaped the problem statement and the three-level system.
